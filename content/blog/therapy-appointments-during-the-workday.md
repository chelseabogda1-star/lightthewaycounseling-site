Almost everyone who calls a therapy office asks for the same thing: an evening appointment, or a Saturday. Which is why those are the hardest slots in the entire schedule to get, often with waits measured in months.

Meanwhile, the 10am and 1pm slots frequently sit open.

If you've been told there's a long wait, it's worth asking a different question: *what do you have during the day?*

## The math nobody mentions

A practice has a handful of after-five slots per clinician per week, and effectively everyone wants them. Ask for 6pm on a Tuesday and you're in a queue. Ask for 11am on a Wednesday and you may be able to start next week.

That difference — starting now versus starting in the spring — usually matters more than the inconvenience of the hour.

## Telehealth changed what "fitting it in" means

A virtual session removes the commute on both ends. That turns a two-hour errand into a 50-minute block, which is a very different thing to ask of a workday. In practice that means:

- A lunch-hour session from your car or a booked conference room
- Starting at 8am before the day gets loud
- 4pm on a day you're working from home
- Sessions that survive travel, weather, and a sick kid at home

Illinois clinicians can see clients located anywhere in the state, so a Batavia practice is a realistic option whether you're in Geneva, Aurora, or working downtown.

## Making a daytime session actually work

**Block it like a meeting.** On the calendar, recurring, marked busy. "Appointment" is a complete explanation; you do not owe anyone more detail.

**Protect fifteen minutes on the back end.** Going straight from a hard session into a status call is rough. A short buffer to sit in the car or take a walk does most of the work.

**Solve the privacy problem honestly.** A parked car is the most common therapy office in America. Headphones, a laptop, a corner of a lot. A booked room with a door works too, if it has one.

**Use your benefits.** Many employers offer an EAP with a handful of covered sessions, and some plans cover telehealth identically to in-person visits. Worth twenty minutes of reading before you assume cost is the barrier.

**Anchor it to something.** Same day, same time, every week. Appointments that move around are the ones that quietly disappear.

## If daytime genuinely isn't possible

Some jobs don't bend — shift work, classrooms, floors you can't leave. In that case, get on the waitlist for the evening slot *and* ask whether the practice keeps a cancellation list. Late cancellations happen constantly, and people on the list often get seen much faster than the official wait suggests.
